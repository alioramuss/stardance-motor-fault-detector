## Added to the dashboard

Three things on top of yesterday's live detector:

- **A live frequency spectrum.** 256-point FFT with a Hann window, so you can
  watch the 37.5 Hz rotation peak in real time. It shows the peak in Hz and rpm,
  and turns red when the verdict is FAULT.
- **A physical alarm.** The browser now sends `F` or `H` back down the serial
  line and the Arduino lights its onboard LED. So the fault shows up on the
  hardware, not just on screen.
- **An adjustable alarm threshold**, with 15 points of hysteresis so a marginal
  signal can't flicker between states. Lowering it catches faults earlier at the
  cost of the odd false alarm — which is usually the right trade for an alarm,
  because a missed failure costs more than a false one.

## Tried adding a second sensor, and it didn't work out

The idea: use an HC-SR04 ultrasonic rangefinder as a **non-contact** channel
alongside the accelerometer, with the servo sweeping it to find the best angle.
Can a £2 rangefinder see a fault that a contact sensor sees?

Most of the afternoon went on getting no echo at all. Two causes, both stupid,
both worth writing down:

1. **Trig and Echo were swapped.** Echo has to be on D2 or D3 on an Uno,
   because those are the only pins that can raise the interrupt I'm using to
   time the pulse without blocking.
2. **The breadboard power rail wasn't connected to anything.** I'd plugged the
   sensor's VCC into the + rail, but nothing was feeding the rail from the Uno.
   Then when I moved it next to the 5V wire it still didn't work — because holes
   *side by side* on a breadboard are separate circuits. Only the group of five
   running across is one connection. I'd been building on that misunderstanding
   for a while.

Once powered it worked immediately — 112 mm, ±1 mm of noise, 0% missed echoes.

Then the servo never turned, so all five "angles" in my sweep were the same
physical position. Ran both conditions anyway.

## Why I threw the result away

The numbers looked brilliant. Ultrasonic spread went from 1.2 mm healthy to
12.6 mm faulty — a 10.7× separation. Better than I expected.

Then I checked the accelerometer, which was recording at the same time as a
reference. Vibration during the "healthy" run was sd **52**, against **3832**
for faulty. My earlier healthy recordings with the fan definitely running were
around 2500.

**The fan was switched off during the healthy run.** So the 10.7× wasn't
balanced versus unbalanced — it was fan-off versus fan-on. The result was
meaningless.

Recording both sensors simultaneously is the only reason I caught it. If I'd
only logged the ultrasonic I'd have written up a fake result and believed it.

The raw distances say something else too: the faulty readings scatter from 65 to
216 mm, which isn't sub-millimetre housing wobble — that's the blades chopping
through the beam. Real effect, but not the one I was testing for.

Parking the ultrasonic here. The core accelerometer project works and I'd rather
finish that properly.
